# Define the beginner promise

## Question

What should a beginner gain from this system that they do not get from their existing camera app, and what trade-offs should govern the first experience?

Use the owner's provisional priorities: AI chooses capture settings and explains them; scenic photography is the starting context; people and selfies remain relevant. Distinguish improved captured data, confidence at the shutter, and learning photographic concepts. Discuss concrete situations and agree what success and failure look like without choosing an implementation or excluding future use cases.

## Comments

### Beginner priorities from the owner

- Audience: beginners.
- Lead benefit: get the shot, with learning available when wanted.
- Do not lock the output workflow yet: compare a usable-photo-plus-preserved-RAW experience with an experience centred on later RAW editing.
- Scenic situations remain the initial emphasis, with people and selfies relevant and no permanent motion exclusion.
- A proposed plan with an explanation is a candidate interaction; alternatives remain open.

Still being discussed: whether similar photo quality with clearer capture choices and explanations is sufficient, whether improved captured data/editability is required, or whether both sources of value should be tested before committing. No resolution is recorded yet.

### Owner's answer on value, 2026-10-06

Explore both capture improvements and clearer decision-making before deciding whether improved captured data is essential.

## Answer

The starting audience is beginner photographers. The lead promise is to help them get the shot through deliberate capture choices, with detailed learning and explanations available when wanted. The owner later proposed slower capture aimed at useful data as the working default, with quick capture optional; short feedback may be part of that default. Scenic photography in New Zealand is the initial emphasis; people and selfies remain relevant, and motion-heavy situations are not permanently excluded.

Investigate two sources of value before choosing a product direction: improvements in useful captured data or RAW editability, and clearer, more reliable capture decisions with understandable reasoning. The owner has not made superiority over Samsung Camera an entry requirement, nor accepted similar image quality as sufficient. Those are hypotheses for comparison.

Compare both output workflows: an immediately usable photo with preserved RAW, and a workflow centred on later RAW editing. A proposed capture plan with explanation and override is one candidate interaction; innovative alternatives remain open. No specific model, framework, algorithm, rendering pipeline, or final interaction is selected.

Choose capture trials and evidence thresholds will decide the comparison situations and success criteria. Explore capture and explanation interactions will provide concrete alternatives for the owner to judge. Actual hardware behavior remains the responsibility of the device-evidence task.

### Owner refinement on default capture, 2026-10-06

The owner expects a mix of handheld and tripod use. They question the value of another app whose default is quick capture and propose a slower default aimed at useful captured data, possibly with feedback, with quick capture optional. Treat this as a working preference, consistent with their early exploration, rather than a fixed delay or a guaranteed data improvement.

This refines the beginner promise toward deliberate photography. Detailed learning remains optional. What feedback means and when it appears still require discussion; it must not be silently equated with a mandatory tutorial.
